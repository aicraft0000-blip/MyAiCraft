50 Social Media Hooks That Stop the Scroll

Proven Opening Lines for LinkedIn, X, and Instagram

The first line of your post determines whether anyone reads the rest. A strong hook earns you 3 seconds of attention. A weak one gets you scrolled past in 0.5 seconds. This guide gives you 50 hooks organised by category, each with an example post showing how to use it. Steal them. Adapt them. Make them yours.

How to Use These Hooks

Each hook follows this format:

  • The Hook Template — the opening line structure
  • Example Post — a complete post using that hook, written for a small business or freelance audience

You don't need to use these word-for-word. Swap in your industry, your numbers, your experience. The structure is what matters.

A note on platforms: These hooks work across LinkedIn, X, and Instagram captions. LinkedIn favours longer posts (800–1,500 characters). X works best short (under 280 characters for the hook, thread for the rest). Instagram captions can go long but the first line must hook before the "…more" cutoff.

Category 1: Storytelling Hooks

These pull readers in with narrative. People are wired for stories.

Hook #1: "I was [bad situation]. Then [unexpected thing happened]."

Example post

I was £4,000 in debt with zero clients. Then a stranger's LinkedIn comment changed everything.

In March 2023, I posted about my freelance copywriting services. No one engaged. But one person left a comment: "Your writing is good but your positioning is terrible." That stung. But she was right.

I spent a weekend rewriting my profile from "I write copy" to "I help SaaS startups turn free trial users into paying customers." Within 6 weeks, I had 4 clients and was charging £800 per project instead of £150.

The lesson: specificity sells. "I do everything" means "I do nothing" to a potential client.

Hook #2: "Nobody talks about [hidden truth in your industry]."

Example post

Nobody talks about the first 6 months of freelancing.

The client who ghosts you after you send the invoice. The project that takes 40 hours when you quoted for 10. The Sunday nights wondering if you made a massive mistake. I went through all of it in 2022. Two things got me through: a £2,000 emergency fund that bought me time, and a weekly accountability call with another freelancer who was in the same boat.

If you're in that first 6 months right now, know this: it gets easier. Not because the work gets simpler, but because you get faster at saying no to bad projects.

Hook #3: "The best [advice/decision] I ever [received/made] cost me [£0/nothing/5 minutes]."

Example post

The best business advice I ever received cost me nothing.

A client told me: "Stop sending proposals. Start sending results." So instead of writing "I can help you with social media," I started sending potential clients a free content calendar for their first week — five posts, written and ready to go. My close rate went from 15% to 60% in a single month.

Hook #4: "Three years ago, I [past situation]. Today, I [current reality]."

Example post

Three years ago, I charged £25 for a blog post. Today, I charge £500.

The writing didn't change that much. What changed was who I wrote for and how I positioned the value. At £25, I was writing "500-word blog posts for websites." At £500, I'm writing "SEO content that ranks on page 1 and brings in leads for 12+ months." Same skill. Different frame. Your price is a reflection of the problem you solve, not the hours you spend.

Hook #5: "I almost quit [thing] in [year]. Here's why I didn't."

Example post

I almost quit freelancing in January 2023. Here's why I didn't.

I'd lost my two biggest clients in the same week. One went in-house, the other cut their budget. My income dropped from £3,200/month to £800 overnight. I sat down and did the maths. I needed 4 new clients at £600/month to replace that income. I gave myself 60 days. I sent 150 cold emails over 3 weeks. Landed 6 calls. Closed 3 clients. Hit £2,400/month by mid-February. The skill that saved my freelance career wasn't writing or design. It was learning to sell when I didn't feel like selling.

Hook #6: "[Number] years in [industry] taught me [number] things."

Example post

8 years in digital marketing taught me 2 things most business owners get wrong.

First: you don't need to be on every platform. Pick the one where your customers spend time and show up there consistently. A restaurant doesn't need a TikTok strategy. It needs a strong Google Business profile with fresh photos.

Second: the best marketing feels like helping. If every post is "buy my thing," people tune out. If every post teaches something useful, people start trusting you. Trust converts better than any ad.

Hook #7: "My biggest mistake as a [role] was [specific mistake]."

Example post

My biggest mistake as a freelancer was saying yes to every project.

In 2021, I took on a branding project, two website builds, and a social media contract in the same month. I was working 14-hour days and delivering mediocre work to everyone. One client left a negative review. Another asked for a refund. Now I have a rule: three active projects maximum. If a fourth comes in, it goes on a waitlist. My reviews improved, my stress dropped, and my income went up because I could charge more for better work.

Hook #8: "I spent [time/money] on [thing] so you don't have to."

Example post

I spent £2,400 on online courses in 2022 so you don't have to.

Of the 12 courses I bought, only 2 were worth the money. The rest were 90% motivation, 10% information. The 2 that worked had something in common: specific, actionable steps with templates I could copy and adapt the same day. Before you buy any course, ask: "Will I have something tangible to use within 24 hours of finishing this?" If the answer isn't clear, keep your money.

Hook #9: "What I wish someone told me before I [started X]."

Example post

What I wish someone told me before I started an online business:

Your first version will be embarrassing. Launch it anyway. My first website had a stock photo of a handshake on the homepage. I cringe thinking about it. But it brought in my first 3 clients. Revenue is not profit. I made £8,000 in my first 6 months and kept about £2,100 after tools, software, ads, and an accountant. Both lessons cost me months of stress. Take them for free.

Hook #10: "The [thing] that changed my [business/career] wasn't what you'd expect."

Example post

The thing that changed my business wasn't a new strategy or a viral post. It was a spreadsheet.

I started tracking every hour I worked, what I did, and how much revenue each activity generated. After 30 days, the data was brutal. I was spending 60% of my time on admin tasks that generated £0. And 15% on the work that brought in 80% of my income: creating content and outreach. I hired a VA for £12/hour to handle admin. My income went up 40% in 2 months because I spent more time on the work that paid. Track your time for one week. The numbers will surprise you.

Category 2: Data and Numbers Hooks

Numbers stop the scroll because they're specific and credible.

Hook #11: "[Specific number]% of [group] don't know [fact]."

Example post

73% of small businesses don't have a single blog post on their website.

That's 73% of businesses invisible to anyone searching Google for their services. A plumber in Manchester with 5 blog posts about common plumbing problems will outrank every competitor who relies on their homepage alone. You don't need 100 articles. You need 5 that answer the questions your customers type into Google.

Hook #12: "I [tracked/measured] [thing] for [time]. Here are the numbers."

Example post

I tracked my LinkedIn engagement for 90 days. Here are the numbers.

Posts with a personal story: 3.2% average engagement rate. Posts sharing tips or frameworks: 2.1%. Posts promoting my services: 0.4%. The posts where I sold nothing generated 8x more profile views than the ones where I pitched. Stop selling in every post. Start helping. The sales come from the DMs, not the feed.

Hook #13: "It took me [number] [days/attempts] to [achieve thing]."

Example post

It took me 47 cold emails to land my first freelance client.

Emails 1–20: no responses. I was sending generic "I can help with your marketing" messages. Emails 21–35: 3 responses, no closes. I'd improved my subject lines but my pitch was still about me, not about them. Emails 36–47: 5 responses, 2 calls, 1 client. I started each email with a specific observation about their business and one concrete suggestion. Cold email works. But the first version of your email won't. Keep refining.

Hook #14: "The difference between [£X] and [£Y] is [specific thing]."

Example post

The difference between a £500/month freelancer and a £5,000/month freelancer is positioning.

At £500/month, you're competing on price with thousands of generalists. At £5,000/month, you're the person who "helps B2B SaaS companies reduce churn through onboarding email sequences." There are maybe 30 people positioned that specifically, and companies pay a premium because the value is clear. Niche down. It feels scary. It works.

Hook #15: "I analysed [number] [things] and found [pattern]."

Example post

I analysed 200 of my Instagram posts over 2 years and found a clear pattern.

Posts with faces got 2.4x more engagement than posts without. Carousel posts got shared 3x more than single images. Posting between 7–8am on weekdays outperformed every other time slot. The algorithm isn't mysterious. It rewards content people engage with. And people engage with faces, useful carousels, and content that appears when they're commuting.

Hook #16: "[Thing] costs [£X]. [Not doing thing] costs [£Y]."

Example post

A professional logo costs £300–£500. Looking unprofessional costs you every client who judges your business in the first 3 seconds.

I used a Canva logo for my first year. When I hired a designer, my enquiry-to-client conversion rate went from 20% to 35%. That 15% improvement paid for the logo in the first week. Invest in the things customers see first: your logo, your website homepage, your Google Business photos.

Hook #17: "I made [£X] in [timeframe]. Here's the breakdown."

Example post

I made £4,200 last month as a solo freelancer. Here's where it came from.

2 website copywriting projects: £1,800. 1 email sequence: £600. Monthly retainer client (social media captions): £500. Monthly retainer client (blog posts): £800. One-off consultation call: £500. The retainers cover my bills. The projects build savings. The consultation funds my learning budget. Multiple income streams within freelancing is safer than relying on one big client.

Hook #18: "Only [small number] of [things] matter. Here they are."

Example post

Only 2 metrics matter for your small business website: how many visitors turn into enquiries (your conversion rate), and how many enquiries turn into customers (your close rate).

If you get 1,000 visitors and 10 enquiries, your conversion rate is 1%. Fix your website copy, add testimonials, and make your contact form shorter. Aim for 3%. If you close 2 of those 10 enquiries, your close rate is 20%. Improve your follow-up speed and your proposal template. Everything else is vanity.

Hook #19: "[Popular advice] is wrong. The data shows [opposite]."

Example post

"Post every day" is wrong. The data shows consistency beats frequency.

I tested two approaches over 60 days. Days 1–30: posted daily on LinkedIn. Average engagement: 1.8%. I burned out by day 22 and started posting filler. Days 31–60: posted 3x per week (Monday, Wednesday, Friday). Average engagement: 3.4%. I had time to write better content. Three excellent posts beat seven mediocre ones. Every time.

Hook #20: "The ROI on [specific action] was [specific result]."

Example post

The ROI on a £40/month email marketing tool was £2,800 in 4 months.

I set up a simple 5-email welcome sequence for new subscribers. No fancy design. No complex automation. Five plain-text emails that delivered value and made one offer. 200 people went through the sequence. 14 bought my £200 service package. That's £2,800 from a system I built once and haven't touched since. Email marketing is boring. Boring things that make money are worth your attention.

Category 3: Contrarian Hooks

Challenge popular thinking. Disagreement creates engagement.

Hook #21: "Stop [common advice everyone gives]."

Example post

Stop trying to go viral.

Viral posts bring followers who don't care about what you sell. One of my posts hit 50,000 views. It brought 400 new followers. Zero clients. My posts that get 2,000 views? Those bring 1–2 client enquiries per month. Because they're targeted at the exact people who need my services. Reach is vanity. Relevance is revenue.

Hook #22: "[Common belief] is the worst advice for [your audience]."

Example post

"Follow your passion" is the worst advice for new freelancers.

Follow the money first. Then build passion around it. I'm not passionate about writing email sequences. But I'm passionate about earning £2,000/month from my home office while picking up my kids from school at 3pm. Find what people will pay for. Get good at it. The passion grows from competence and freedom, not the other way around.

Hook #23: "Unpopular opinion: [statement that goes against the grain]."

Example post

Unpopular opinion: most small businesses don't need a social media presence.

They need a Google Business profile with 5-star reviews, a website that loads fast and answers common questions, and a way to collect email addresses from interested visitors. Social media is free to use but expensive in time. If you're a local plumber, spending 10 hours a week on Instagram is a worse investment than spending 2 hours asking happy customers for Google reviews. Match your marketing to your business model.

Hook #24: "Everyone's doing [thing]. Nobody's doing [better thing]."

Example post

Everyone's building a social media following. Nobody's building an email list.

Your Instagram followers aren't yours. Meta changes the algorithm and your reach drops 70% overnight (it happened in 2023). Your email list is yours. No algorithm. No platform risk. And email converts at 3–5%, while social media converts at 0.5–1%. Start collecting emails today, even if your list is tiny. 100 email subscribers are worth more than 5,000 Instagram followers.

Hook #25: "The [thing] nobody wants to hear about [topic]."

Example post

The truth nobody wants to hear about starting a business: it takes longer than you think.

The average timeline from "idea" to "consistent monthly income" is 12–18 months. Not 30 days. Not 90 days. I know because I tracked 40 people in a business community I'm part of. The ones who are earning well in year two all spent year one building, failing, and rebuilding. If you're in month 6 and frustrated, you're on schedule. Keep going.

Hook #26: "I stopped [popular tactic] and [positive result happened]."

Example post

I stopped posting motivational quotes and my LinkedIn engagement tripled.

For 6 months, I mixed "inspirational" content with practical advice. The quotes got likes. The practical posts got comments, DMs, and clients. Likes are empty calories. Comments are conversations. Conversations become clients. Now I post two types of content: lessons from my experience and tactical breakdowns people can use the same day.

Hook #27: "Your [thing] isn't the problem. Your [other thing] is."

Example post

Your product isn't the problem. Your offer is.

I worked with a web designer charging £800 for a website. She was getting ghosted after sending quotes. We didn't change her skills or her portfolio. We changed her offer. Old offer: "Custom website design — £800." New offer: "5-page website, delivered in 14 days, with SEO setup and 30 days of post-launch support — £1,200." She raised her price by £400 and started closing 3x more clients. Because the offer was clear, specific, and included things people valued.

Hook #28: "[Tool/Platform everyone uses] is overrated. Here's what I use instead."

Example post

Notion is overrated for freelancers. Here's what I use instead.

A single Google Sheet with four tabs: Tab 1: Active projects (client, deadline, status, payment). Tab 2: Pipeline (leads, last contact date, next action). Tab 3: Finances (income, expenses, profit by month). Tab 4: Content (post ideas, publish date, performance). It takes 10 minutes to set up. Zero learning curve. No templates to customise. The best system is the one you'll use every day. For most solo operators, that's a spreadsheet.

Hook #29: "The [industry] industry doesn't want you to know [truth]."

Example post

The marketing industry doesn't want you to know that most of what works is boring.

The exciting stuff — viral campaigns, influencer partnerships, TikTok trends — accounts for maybe 5% of results for small businesses. The boring stuff — emailing your list weekly, posting useful content consistently, asking for reviews, following up with leads — accounts for 90%. Do the boring things well. That's the strategy.

Hook #30: "If your [thing] isn't [doing Y], the problem isn't [obvious cause]."

Example post

If your website isn't getting enquiries, the problem isn't your design. It's your copy.

Most small business websites look fine. But the homepage says "Welcome to Our Company" instead of "We help [specific person] get [specific result]." Swap your headline from a greeting to a value statement. Add one clear call-to-action per page. Include a testimonial within the first scroll. I've seen these 3 changes double enquiry rates on sites that hadn't been redesigned at all.

Category 4: Question Hooks

Questions pull readers in because the brain automatically tries to answer them.

Hook #31: "What would you do with an extra [specific amount] per month?"

Example post

What would you do with an extra £1,000 per month?

That's 2 freelance clients paying £500 each. Or 100 sales of a £10 digital product. Or one retainer client. The gap between £0 and £1,000/month isn't talent — it's specificity. You need a clear service, a clear price, and a clear audience. "I do graphic design" won't get you there. "I create social media graphics for fitness coaches — 20 posts for £500/month" will.

Hook #32: "How many [things] did you [do] this week?"

Example post

How many conversations did you start with potential clients this week?

Not posts. Not content. Conversations. The freelancers I know who earn £3,000+ per month all do the same thing: they start 10–15 conversations per week with people who might need their services. LinkedIn DMs, email outreach, referral asks. Content builds awareness. Conversations close deals. If the answer to my question is "zero," that's where to focus this week.

Hook #33: "What's the one thing you'd change about your [business/strategy]?"

Example post

What's the one thing you'd change about how you find clients?

I asked 50 freelancers this question. The top two answers: "I'd start outreach sooner instead of waiting for clients to find me." And "I'd raise my prices and work with fewer, better clients." Both answers have something in common: they require doing the uncomfortable thing now instead of the comfortable thing later. What's yours?

Hook #34: "Are you making this [common] mistake with your [thing]?"

Example post

Are you making this common mistake with your pricing?

Most freelancers set their rate by looking at what others charge and picking a number in the middle. You have no idea what those other freelancers' expenses, skills, or client quality look like. You're pricing blind. Try this instead: calculate your monthly expenses (personal + business). Add your desired savings. Divide by the number of hours you can bill per month (hint: it's about 60% of your working hours). That's your minimum hourly rate. Anything below it and you're losing money.

Hook #35: "When was the last time you [important action most people neglect]?"

Example post

When was the last time you followed up with a past client?

I keep a list of every client I've worked with. Every 90 days, I send a short email: "Hope the business is going well. I wanted to share [useful article/tip related to their industry]. If you need anything on the [my service] front, I'm here." 40% of my annual revenue comes from repeat clients. And it starts with a 2-minute email every quarter. Set a calendar reminder. Today.

Hook #36: "Why do [successful group] always [specific behaviour]?"

Example post

Why do the best-earning freelancers always say no to rush jobs?

Because rush jobs break your schedule, stress you out, and train clients to expect last-minute turnarounds. A £500 rush job that costs you 2 days of disrupted work on other projects isn't worth £500. It's costing you the £1,000 you'd have earned from your planned work, plus the quality hit on everything else. If you do accept rush work, charge 50–100% more. The premium covers the chaos.

Hook #37: "Would you rather have [option A] or [option B]?"

Example post

Would you rather have 10,000 followers or 1,000 email subscribers?

I'd take the email list every time. 1,000 email subscribers at a 3% conversion rate on a £100 product = £3,000. 10,000 followers at a 0.3% conversion rate on the same product = £3,000. Looks equal on paper. But your email list doesn't depend on an algorithm. You can reach them whenever you want. Build the list.

Hook #38: "What if [common assumption] isn't true?"

Example post

What if you don't need more traffic to make more money?

Most businesses I work with don't have a traffic problem. They have a conversion problem. 1,000 visitors with a 1% conversion rate = 10 customers. Same 1,000 visitors with a 3% conversion rate = 30 customers. You tripled your revenue without a single extra visitor. Before you spend money on ads or time on content, fix your website's ability to turn visitors into buyers. Better headlines, clearer calls to action, and visible testimonials go a long way.

Hook #39: "What would [specific expert/successful person] do differently?"

Example post

What would your most successful competitor do differently with your website?

They'd probably cut 70% of the text on your homepage. They'd put a testimonial above the fold. They'd make the call-to-action button bigger and use action words ("Get Your Free Quote" instead of "Submit"). You don't need a redesign. You need 30 minutes of honest editing. Open your homepage right now. Read it as if you're a customer who's never heard of you. Does it answer "what do you do, who do you do it for, and why should I pick you" in 10 seconds? If not, rewrite until it does.

Hook #40: "How much is [common problem] costing you?"

Example post

How much is your slow website costing you?

Google's data says 53% of mobile visitors leave a site that takes more than 3 seconds to load. If your site gets 500 visitors per month and half leave before it loads, that's 250 people who never see your offer. At a 3% conversion rate, that's 7–8 lost customers per month. Go to PageSpeed Insights (free, by Google), test your site, and check your mobile speed score. If it's below 50, contact your web host or developer. Often the fix is compressing images and removing plugins you don't use.

Category 5: Personal and Vulnerability Hooks

Showing your human side builds trust faster than any polished post.

Hook #41: "I'll be honest: [admission that shows vulnerability]."

Example post

I'll be honest: I still get nervous before client calls.

After 5 years and 200+ clients, the first 30 seconds of a new call still make my stomach flip. What's changed isn't the nerves. It's what I do with them. I prepare 3 specific observations about their business before every call. I write down my first sentence so I don't stumble. And I remind myself: they booked this call because they need help. If you're nervous about sales calls, that's normal. Prepare more than you think you need to, and the nerves become fuel instead of friction.

Hook #42: "The thing I'm most proud of isn't [obvious achievement]."

Example post

The thing I'm most proud of in my business isn't my revenue or my client list. It's the fact that I haven't missed a school pickup in 18 months.

I started freelancing because I wanted time freedom. For the first year, I worked more hours than I did in my corporate job. The freedom felt like a lie. Then I set two non-negotiable rules: no calls after 2:30pm, and no work on weekends. My income dipped for one month while I adjusted. Then it recovered because I was more focused during my working hours. Set your boundaries before you burn out. Not after.

Hook #43: "I lost [specific thing] because I [specific mistake]. Here's what I learned."

Example post

I lost a £6,000 client because I didn't have a contract. Here's what I learned.

They were my biggest client. We worked on a handshake agreement for 8 months. Then they brought someone in-house, gave them all my templates and processes, and stopped replying to my emails. No contract meant no notice period, no intellectual property protection, and no final payment for the last month's work. Now every client signs a contract before I start. It covers scope, payment terms, notice period, and IP ownership. Use a contract. Every time. No exceptions.

Hook #44: "I used to believe [old belief]. Now I know [new understanding]."

Example post

I used to believe I needed to be the cheapest option to win clients. Now I know the opposite is true.

When I charged £30/hour, clients questioned everything: "Why did that take 3 hours?" "Can you do this faster?" When I moved to project pricing at £500–£1,500 per project, the questions stopped. Higher-paying clients trust your expertise more and micromanage less. Cheap clients cost you the most. In time, in energy, and in the work you can't take on because you're drowning in low-value projects.

Hook #45: "The hardest part of [your work] isn't [what people think]. It's [unexpected reality]."

Example post

The hardest part of running a business isn't finding clients or doing the work. It's making decisions alone.

Should I raise my prices? Should I fire this difficult client? Should I invest in ads or save the money? There's no boss to ask. No team to discuss it with. And every wrong decision comes out of your own pocket. Two things help: a monthly check-in with another business owner who's at your level (not a guru, a peer). And a simple decision framework — will this matter in 12 months? If yes, think carefully. If no, decide in 5 minutes and move on.

Hook #46: "This is what [number] [days/months/years] of [thing] looks like."

Example post

This is what 3 years of freelancing looks like in real numbers:

Year 1: £18,000 revenue. Worked 50-hour weeks. Felt overwhelmed constantly. Year 2: £38,000 revenue. Worked 40-hour weeks. Fired 2 bad clients. Raised prices twice. Year 3: £52,000 revenue. Worked 30-hour weeks. Built recurring income. Took 4 weeks off. The jump from year 1 to year 3 wasn't about working harder. It was about raising prices, choosing better clients, and building systems so I didn't redo the same work every month.

Hook #47: "A client once told me [surprising/useful feedback]."

Example post

A client once told me: "I hired you because of your follow-up, not your portfolio."

She said three other freelancers had better portfolios. But I was the only one who replied to her enquiry within 2 hours and sent a follow-up email the next day with ideas specific to her business. That's it. Speed and specificity beat talent when talent doesn't show up. If you're not winning clients, check your response time. Are enquiries sitting in your inbox for 48 hours? That's 48 hours for someone else to close the deal.

Hook #48: "Here's what my [morning/weekly] routine looks like (and why it works)."

Example post

Here's what my Monday morning routine looks like (and why it works).

7:30am — Review my project list. Identify the 2 tasks that will move the needle most this week. 8:00am — Client work. No email, no social media. 3 hours of focused delivery. 11:00am — Respond to emails and messages from the weekend. 11:30am — 30 minutes of outreach: follow up with leads, send 3 new cold emails, comment on 5 potential clients' posts. By noon on Monday, I've done my most important client work and my most important business development. The rest of the week feels calmer because the high-impact work is already done.

Hook #49: "If I had to start over with [£X/nothing], here's what I'd do."

Example post

If I had to start my freelance business over with £100, here's what I'd spend it on:

£0 — Set up profiles on LinkedIn and one freelance platform (both free). £16 — One month of ChatGPT Plus for writing drafts and proposals faster. £0 — Build a basic portfolio on Carrd (free tier) with 3 sample projects. £84 — Saved as a buffer for the first invoice payment delay (which will happen). Everything else — client outreach, networking, building skills — costs time, not money. The myth that you need thousands to start is a myth. You need a laptop, an internet connection, and 10 hours a week of focused effort.

Hook #50: "One year from now, you'll wish you had started today."

Example post

One year from now, you'll wish you had started today.

Not because it's easy. But because most of the hard part is the beginning. Once you have your first client, the second is easier. Once you've earned your first £1,000, the path to £5,000 is clearer. The person who starts imperfectly today is further ahead in 12 months than the person who waits for the perfect plan. Pick one thing from this guide. Do it before midnight. Your future self will thank you.
