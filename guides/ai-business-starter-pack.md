## Prompt 1 — Client Proposal

**When to use it:** You need to write a proposal for a new client and you want it to feel professional, specific, and persuasive without spending two hours on it.

**The prompt:**
```
You are writing a business proposal on my behalf.

My business: [describe what you do in 2 sentences]
The client: [name of client and what they do]
What they need: [the problem or project they've described]
What I'm proposing to do: [your solution or service]
Price: [your fee or pricing structure]
Timeline: [when you'd deliver or start]

Write a professional proposal with these sections:
1. Opening — acknowledge their situation and what they're trying to achieve
2. Our approach — how I would tackle this specifically
3. What's included — a clear list of deliverables
4. Investment — the price with a brief justification
5. Next step — one clear action for them to take

Tone: confident, direct, no jargon. Write as if you know the client reasonably well — warm but professional. Maximum 400 words.
```

**Why it works:** Giving the AI both sides of the conversation (your business AND the client's situation) means it can write something specific rather than generic. The section structure prevents waffle.

## Prompt 2 — Complaint Response

**When to use it:** A customer is unhappy and you need to respond in a way that's genuine, takes responsibility where appropriate, and keeps the relationship intact.

**The prompt:**
```
A customer has complained and I need to write a response.

What they complained about: [describe the complaint]
What actually happened from my side: [your honest account]
What I'm prepared to do about it: [refund, redo, discount, apology only — be specific]

Write a response that:
- Opens by acknowledging their experience (not dismissing it)
- Explains what happened briefly and honestly — no deflecting
- States clearly what I'm doing to resolve it
- Closes in a way that gives them a reason to stay

Tone: genuine, calm, not defensive. Under 200 words. No corporate language ("we apologise for any inconvenience" is banned).
```

**Why it works:** Banning specific phrases forces the AI away from the canned responses that make customers angrier. The honest account section stops the AI from making promises you can't keep.

## Prompt 3 — Cold Outreach Email

**When to use it:** You want to reach out to a potential client, partner, or collaborator and you don't want to sound like everyone else.

**The prompt:**
```
Write a cold outreach email for me.

Who I'm contacting: [name, role, company]
Why I'm contacting them specifically: [something specific about them — a post they wrote, a problem their business has, a recent announcement]
What I do: [one clear sentence]
What I'm asking for: [a call, a reply, a specific question — keep it one thing]

Rules:
- Subject line must be under 8 words and not use the word "quick"
- First sentence must reference something specific about them — not a generic compliment
- Body must be under 100 words
- One clear ask at the end — not multiple options
- No "I hope this finds you well" or "I wanted to reach out"
```

**Why it works:** Cold emails fail because they're about the sender. These constraints force the email to lead with the recipient's world before introducing yours.

## Prompt 4 — Meeting Summary

**When to use it:** You've just finished a call or meeting and need to send a summary with clear actions.

**The prompt:**
```
Turn these rough meeting notes into a clean summary.

Meeting notes:
[paste your notes — even messy bullet points or half-sentences work]

Attendees: [names and roles]
Date: [date]

Format the output as:
- 3-sentence overview of what was discussed and decided
- Action items: each on its own line, with the owner's name and a deadline if mentioned
- Any open questions or things left unresolved
- Suggested date for follow-up if relevant

Keep the whole thing under 300 words. Plain language only.
```

**Why it works:** Messy notes → clean output. The structured format means you can paste it straight into an email or project tool.

## Prompt 5 — Team Update

**When to use it:** You need to write a weekly or monthly update to your team, stakeholders, or investors and you want it to be honest and useful rather than a list of what you did.

**The prompt:**
```
Write a team/stakeholder update for me.

Period: [week/month and dates]
What went well: [bullet points — even rough]
What didn't go as planned: [be honest here — AI won't judge]
What I'm focused on next: [priorities for the coming period]
Any decisions I need input on: [optional]

Format: short opening paragraph (2 sentences), then three sections — Wins, Challenges, What's Next. End with one honest sentence about where things stand overall.

Tone: direct, no spin. This is for people who need to know what's actually happening, not a PR exercise. Under 250 words.
```

**Why it works:** The "no spin" instruction keeps the AI from inflating good news and burying bad news — which is what makes these updates actually trusted.

## Prompt 6 — Competitor Analysis

**When to use it:** You want to understand how a competitor is positioning themselves and where they're vulnerable.

**The prompt:**
```
Analyse this competitor for me.

Competitor: [name and website if you have it]
My business: [what you do and who you serve]

I want to understand:
1. How they position themselves — what problem do they claim to solve and for whom
2. What they charge and how their pricing is structured
3. Where their offer looks strong
4. Where their offer looks weak or incomplete
5. What a customer choosing them over me is probably prioritising

Be direct. I'm not looking for a balanced academic summary — I want to know where the gap is that I can exploit.
```

**Why it works:** Asking for the exploitable gap rather than a balanced view forces the AI to give you something actionable rather than a Wikipedia entry.

## Prompt 7 — Customer Research Summary

**When to use it:** You have reviews, feedback, or conversations and want to understand what your customers are actually saying beneath the surface.

**The prompt:**
```
Analyse this customer feedback and tell me what it's really saying.

Feedback source: [reviews / support emails / survey responses / interview notes]

Paste the feedback here:
[paste up to 20 reviews or comments]

I want to know:
1. The 3 main things customers love — in their own language, not marketing language
2. The 3 main frustrations or unmet needs
3. The specific words and phrases they use that I should be using in my own copy
4. One thing this feedback suggests I should change or add
5. One thing it suggests I should stop doing or saying

Don't summarise what I already gave you. Tell me what's underneath it.
```

**Why it works:** "In their own language" and "what's underneath it" pull the AI away from paraphrasing and toward genuine insight.

## Prompt 8 — Pricing Sense-Check

**When to use it:** You're not sure if your pricing is right and you want an honest outside perspective.

**The prompt:**
```
I want an honest assessment of my pricing.

What I sell: [describe your product or service clearly]
What I currently charge: [your prices]
Who I sell to: [describe your customer — size, budget, sophistication]
My main competitors and their prices (if known): [list them]

Tell me:
1. Whether my pricing looks too low, too high, or roughly right — and why
2. What my pricing signals to a potential customer seeing it for the first time
3. Whether my structure (one price / tiers / per unit / etc.) makes sense for this market
4. One thing I should consider changing

Be direct. Don't tell me what I want to hear.
```

**Why it works:** "Be direct. Don't tell me what I want to hear" genuinely changes AI output — it's less likely to validate whatever you've already done.

## Prompt 9 — Market Gap Finder

**When to use it:** You're thinking about a new product, service, or niche and want to find where the real opportunity is.

**The prompt:**
```
Help me find the gap in this market.

Market or category: [e.g. "online fitness coaching for women over 40" or "accountancy software for freelancers"]

Look at this from three angles:
1. Who is being underserved — a customer type the main players aren't focused on
2. What problem exists that nobody is solving well
3. What the current options get wrong — the frustration that keeps coming up in reviews

For each angle, give me one specific, concrete opportunity — not a vague suggestion. Tell me who I'd be selling to, what I'd be selling, and why the current options aren't enough.
```

**Why it works:** Forcing three separate angles prevents the AI from giving you one obvious answer. "Specific and concrete" stops it from staying vague.

## Prompt 10 — SWOT in Plain English

**When to use it:** You need to think clearly about a decision, a new direction, or the current state of your business.

**The prompt:**
```
Run a plain-English SWOT analysis on this.

The situation: [describe the business decision, opportunity, or challenge you're assessing]

Context about my business: [2-3 sentences about what you do, your size, your market]

Rules:
- No bullet point padding — every point must be a real, specific observation
- Maximum 3 points per section
- Strengths and Weaknesses should be internal (things I control)
- Opportunities and Threats should be external (things happening in the market)
- End with one sentence: given all of this, what's the most honest single thing I should do next?
```

**Why it works:** "No bullet point padding" is the single most useful instruction you can give an AI on a SWOT. It forces quality over quantity.

## Prompt 11 — Cash Flow Summary

**When to use it:** You want a clear picture of your cash position without having to stare at a spreadsheet for an hour.

**The prompt:**
```
Help me understand my cash position.

Money coming in this month: [list expected income — invoices, subscriptions, one-off payments]
Money going out this month: [list expected expenses — rent, payroll, subscriptions, supplier payments]
Current bank balance: [approximate figure]
Any unusual items: [late payments, large one-off costs, upcoming tax bill]

Tell me:
1. My expected end-of-month balance
2. Whether I have a cash gap risk and when it would hit
3. The one expense or receivable I should focus on this week
4. One question I should be asking myself about this picture

Plain numbers, plain language. No financial jargon.
```

**Why it works:** Most people avoid looking at cash flow because it feels complicated. This prompt produces something you can actually read in 60 seconds.

## Prompt 12 — Invoice Chaser

**When to use it:** A client hasn't paid and you need to chase them without damaging the relationship — or without being a pushover.

**The prompt:**
```
Write an invoice chasing email for me.

Invoice details: [invoice number, amount, original due date]
How overdue: [number of days]
Client relationship: [new client / long-term client / difficult relationship / usually pays on time]
Have I chased before: [yes — how many times / no — this is the first]
Tone I want: [polite reminder / firm / final warning before escalation]

Write one email. Include:
- A clear reference to the invoice (number and amount in the first line)
- The number of days overdue stated plainly
- A specific request for payment by a specific date
- What happens next if it isn't paid (if this is a firm or final email)

No apologising for asking to be paid. No "just" or "only" ("I just wanted to follow up" is banned).
```

**Why it works:** "No apologising for asking to be paid" removes the hedging language that makes invoice chasers easy to ignore.

## Prompt 13 — Contract Red-Flag Scan

**When to use it:** You've received a contract and you want to know what to watch out for before you sign or send it to a solicitor.

**The prompt:**
```
Review this contract and tell me what to watch out for.

Contract type: [employment / freelance / supplier / client / NDA / lease — whichever applies]
My position in this contract: [am I the client, supplier, employee, employer?]

Contract text:
[paste the contract or the sections you're concerned about]

I want:
1. The top 3 clauses I should push back on or negotiate — and why
2. Anything that's missing that should be in a contract like this
3. Any language that's unusually one-sided in the other party's favour
4. A plain-English summary of what I'm actually agreeing to

Note: this is not legal advice. Flag anything serious and I'll take it to a solicitor.
```

**Why it works:** The disclaimer at the end keeps the AI from being overly cautious. The "missing clauses" question catches things that aren't there — which prompts often miss.

## Prompt 14 — SOP From a Voice Note

**When to use it:** You do something regularly that you need to document — for a new hire, a VA, or just to stop doing it from memory.

**The prompt:**
```
Turn this voice note transcript into a Standard Operating Procedure.

What the process is: [name the process — e.g. "onboarding a new client"]
Who will follow this SOP: [employee / VA / contractor / yourself]

Voice note transcript:
[paste your transcript — messy is fine, even "um" and tangents]

Format it as:
- Process name and purpose (1 sentence)
- What you need before you start (tools, access, information)
- Numbered steps — clear and specific enough that someone new could follow them
- What done looks like (how do you know it's complete)
- Common mistakes or things to watch out for

Plain language. If I said something ambiguous, make a reasonable assumption and note it in [brackets] so I can check it.
```

**Why it works:** Most SOPs fail because writing them from scratch takes too long. Speaking for 3 minutes and passing the transcript to AI produces a usable first draft in seconds.

## Prompt 15 — Job Description Writer

**When to use it:** You need to hire someone and you want a job description that attracts the right people and puts off the wrong ones.

**The prompt:**
```
Write a job description for me.

Role title: [job title]
What this person will actually do day-to-day: [be specific — list the main tasks]
What I need them to have: [skills, experience, or qualifications that are non-negotiable]
What would be nice but isn't essential: [desirable extras]
Working arrangement: [full-time / part-time / freelance / remote / office / hybrid]
Salary or rate: [the range — or say "competitive" if you don't want to state it]
What kind of company this is: [2 sentences about your business — size, what you do, culture in one word]

Write it as:
- Short intro about the role and why it exists (2 sentences)
- What you'll be doing (bullet list, no more than 6 points)
- What we're looking for (split into Essential and Desirable)
- What we offer
- How to apply

Do not use: "rockstar", "ninja", "passionate", "fast-paced environment", "must thrive under pressure", or any phrase that makes the company sound more exciting than it is. Honest and direct only.
```

**Why it works:** The banned words list removes the clichés that signal a bad employer to good candidates. Asking what the person will "actually do day-to-day" stops the job description becoming a wish list.
