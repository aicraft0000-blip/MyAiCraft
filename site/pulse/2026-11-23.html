This week I picked three things to know before Christmas, when people shop more, spend more and get more calls from family.

Asking a chatbot where to buy? Check the shop yourself

A study by Podqi, a company that protects brands from fakes, asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity thousands of shopping questions about 151 brands. Risky websites, including fake shops, turned up in 15% of Perplexity's answers, 8% of ChatGPT's and 4% of Gemini's. The chatbots mentioned risky sites far more often than they warned about them, and smaller brands and "local" versions of a shop were the most likely to bring up fakes.

I picked this because a lot of people will ask an AI "where can I buy this for less?" in the next few weeks. Use the answer as a starting point, then go to the shop you know by typing its address yourself. Be wary of prices that look too good, sites you've never heard of, and payment by bank transfer.

Sources: reported by Futurism and Digit. It's one company's study, and I couldn't see its full method, so treat the exact figures as their findings.

A few seconds of someone's voice is enough to copy it

Surrey's Police and Crime Commissioner is warning about cloned-voice scams in the run-up to Christmas, as part of her "Question EVERYTHING" fraud campaign. Her office says criminals can copy a person's voice from as little as three seconds of audio, then use it to ring a relative asking for money in an emergency.

The advice is simple. If a call from someone you love asks for money urgently, hang up and ring them back on the number you already have. Agreeing a family code word in advance helps too, because a cloned voice won't know it.

Sources: Surrey Police and Crime Commissioner: Question EVERYTHING, and reporting of the Christmas warning by Archyde. I couldn't confirm the exact date of the Christmas warning from the commissioner's own site.

Gemini's "temporary" chats are kept for about two months

Gemini has a temporary chat option, meant for things you'd rather not keep. When Google launched it, it said those chats were kept for up to 72 hours. Google's own privacy page now says temporary chats, and chats you have with history switched off, are kept with your account for around two months.

That doesn't make them public, but "temporary" now means weeks, not days. If you've been using it for something sensitive, like a health worry or a money problem, it's worth knowing. For comparison, OpenAI says it may keep temporary ChatGPT chats for up to 30 days.

Sources: Google: Gemini Apps Privacy Hub, Google's launch post for temporary chats. I couldn't find the date the change was made.
